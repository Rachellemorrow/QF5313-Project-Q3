Verified from the stored artifact and its generating code: a deterministic statsmodels probit GLM (Binomial family, probit link) on the origin-row design z_u — the last row of each training window, i.e. the same leak-safe lag-shifted standardized information set the sequence models see at their final month — retaining all 30 panel indicators (no additional screen) plus an intercept. Reported-metrics limitation. Option A stores sensitivity, specificity and balanced accuracy at its fixed 0.5 cutoff; the stored Option B artifacts record Brier, AUC and accuracy only. The Balanced-acc. column is therefore shown for Option A alone (marked in the table headers), and no operating-point measures were recomputed for Option B — its per-seed decision thresholds are part of the trained model selection, and an ad-hoc recomputation from stored probabilities would not reproduce that procedure. Leaky-ablation arms likewise store Brier/AUC/accuracy and are reported only in the ablation section below.

Training-sample differences (verified, not assumed). (i) Option A's primary arm starts at the LPR history (its label sample begins in 2013-11), while Option B exploits the full post-2008 label history. (ii) Option B reserves the most recent 24 label months of each fold as its validation tail for early stopping and threshold selection, so its fit labels at the final fold end in 2024-09 whereas Option A's probit consumes labels through 2026-09. (iii) Both differences are documented in the stored artifacts and reproduced above; nothing was truncated, imputed, or rerun to make the samples equal.

Limitations of direct ranking. Because fit-label windows, lengths, feature representations, and operating points differ, adjacent-model differences in the tables above mix specification effects with sample effects. Option A's factors-only variant is shown as the closest available within-A reference that shares Option B's longer sample persistence while retaining A's factor specification. A matched-training-window robustness experiment (restricting one option's training labels to the other option's window) is a possible future extension; it has not been run, and no numbers for it appear on this page.

3. Leakage ablation (Option B, mandatory)

Design. Three arms under the same 82 folds, labels, seeds and training pipeline as the Option B regime run (lookback h=3): a leak-safe ridge Probit on the origin row of the lag-shifted standardized panel, safe LSTM/GRU whose predictions are reused verbatim from the regime run, and LSTM/GRU trained on the raw unshifted panel (as if every series were published at its reference month-end). For the 21 of 30 series with a non-zero publication lag this feeds the origin row information a real-time forecaster could not have had. The leaky window does not contain the label's second PMI observation, so the demonstration isolates availability leakage of unpublished contemporaneous data rather than direct label peeking.

What the demonstration shows. 4. Clustering — three-layer A/B comparison (interface.md Section 9)

Documented interpretive conflict. assignment.md §5.4.2 asks to "compare clusters with the Option-A correlation clusters (ARI/NMI) and with the regime labels". interface.md §8 and §9 item 8 — a fixed group decision — instead require: "do not calculate direct ARI/NMI between Option A indicator clusters and Option B time-point hidden-state clusters: indicators and time observations are different observation universes", and §10 requires joint member approval to change that rule. Resolution implemented on this page: (1) ARI/NMI is mathematically defined only for two label vectors over the same observations; Option A partitions ~30 indicators (15–21 retained per 36-month window) while Option B partitions 224 month-end time observations in 32-dimensional hidden-state spaces, so no cross-unit ARI/NMI value exists to compute without first inventing a mapping between indicators and time points. (2) The mathematically defined parts of the assignment requirement are fully implemented: Option B's latent clusters are compared with the regime labels via ARI/NMI on identical observations under two explicit alignments (d_t and d_(t+1); Layer 2 below), and within-method stability ARI is computed on both sides (Option A across adjacent windows on the common retained indicators; Option B across seeds on the common 224 time observations). (3) The assignment's analytical intent — a comparison against Option A's cluster structure and the regime labels — is preserved by the ratified three-layer framework. (4) Any future cross-unit quantitative bridge must first be defined mathematically and approved through interface.md §10; nothing of that kind is computed here.

Layer 3 — practical value for economic analysis and contraction monitoring

What Option A provides: a transparent indicator grouping whose correlation geometry is verified, a per-window silhouette/stability record, and representative lead-lag evidence against GDP. The main limitation is that the retained universe is coverage-dependent (about 15-21 indicators per window), and the early windows are smaller than the 30-indicator panel. Cluster count selected by maximum silhouette can choose many small clusters in some windows; the fixed k=4 column is the comparability sensitivity.

What Option B provides: a state-recognition view in time — 224 month-end hidden-state embeddings whose two-state structure shows some alignment with the contraction labels (stored ARI 0.08-0.13 and NMI 0.14-0.22 across architectures and alignments), but the association is limited and the resulting partitions are highly sensitive to initialization — the cluster shares are therefore illustrative of individual initializations — plus honest out-of-sample regime probabilities (Section 2) and the leakage demonstration (Section 3) quantifying what the publication-lag discipline protects. Its main limitation is the low cross-seed stability of the latent partition.

Task-oriented assessment (no aggregate score). For understanding which indicators co-move and how those groups relate to GDP's lead-lag structure, Option A is the direct instrument. For recognizing when the macro system shifts between phases, Option B's latent timeline is the relevant diagnostic, with its regime classifiers supplying the out-of-sample evidence. Neither method yields a stable, definitive taxonomy of the Chinese macroeconomy on the frozen snapshot; both produce suggestive but unstable structure. Following interface.md §9, no single weighted score combining the two methods' unlike metrics is computed.
